Most modern web frameworks bring a lot of ceremony with them. You often start with a simple idea and end up managing complex client-side hydration, bundle configurations, and heavy JavaScript before you've even written your first paragraph.

That's what made trying **Astro** feel like a breath of fresh air.

### 1. HTML by default

Astro sends pure HTML to the browser by default. If your page is mostly content, like a personal portfolio, blog, or documentation, there's rarely a good reason to ship megabytes of JavaScript just to render text and layout. The pages load almost instantly, and performance comes for free.

### 2. Familiar and clean component model

Writing `.astro` components feels remarkably close to writing standard HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. There's no steep learning curve or awkward templating language to master. Everything you need is right in the component file.

### 3. Bring your favorite framework only when needed

You aren't locked into a single ecosystem. If you ever need a rich interactive component, you can bring in React, Svelte, or Vue and hydrate only that specific part using Astro's Islands architecture. The rest of the site stays lightweight and static.

### Keeping things simple

For personal sites and technical writing, less is usually more. Astro strikes a clean balance between a great developer experience and minimal overhead, letting you focus on the content instead of the tooling.
